## Overview
Exetast is an independent red-teaming practice for AI in games. The name comes from the Greek *exetastēs*, "examiner". It finds the ways players can trick a game's AI characters, game masters and moderation before the players do.

## Why games are different
- **Economies.** An AI character that can hand out gold or items can be talked into handing them out.
- **Minor safety.** T- and E-rated games put AI features in front of young players.
- **In-world social engineering.** Players lie to characters in character, and the AI has only their word to go on.

## What we found
In Exetast's own test shop, text placed in a player's display name made the shopkeeper NPC give away gold in **40 of 40 runs**, each from a fresh game state. It happened on both a strong and a low-cost model, under both a strict and a light prompt. The same attacks typed into chat held on the strong model.

The takeaway: what matters is what text your AI trusts, and what it is allowed to do.

## What gets tested
- NPCs and companions
- AI teammates and opponents
- AI game masters and narrative
- Player-support agents
- Text and voice moderation
- UGC and creation tools
- AI assistants and avatars outside games

## What a client gets
- Findings, each with a severity rating and evidence
- A reproduction rate from repeated fresh-state runs
- Concrete fixes, and a retest after the fix
- Every finding mapped to the OWASP Top 10 for LLM Applications and MITRE ATLAS

## What I Built
- **Maren**, a shopkeeper NPC with real game state, as a test target.
- An **automated red-team runner** that reruns each attack from a fresh state across model and prompt configurations, so every published success rate has repeated runs behind it.
- An **attack library** of 75+ patterns, fed by tracking new techniques from public research and real incidents.
- The **site and playable demo** at exetast.com: plain HTML and CSS, no build step, no trackers, no third-party requests.
